Community AI: Recognize Access

Human variation, local governance, and participation as the optimization target

When I walk beside Fort Greene Park, I see many different ways of moving through the same place.

A wheelchair. A motorized chair. A walker. A stroller. Someone moving slowly. Someone running. Someone carrying bags. Someone stopping to rest.

We are all sharing the same environment.

What if AI governance began by asking what access conditions a community needs supported right now?

Community AI starts from that question.

It does not need to categorize people. It can work from community-declared needs, accessibility reports, requested accommodations, route feedback, service requests, and public environmental data.

The goal is not classification. The goal is participation.

Access as a changing state

Let the local access state at time t be:

A(t) = [m, p, r, s, c, l, e, g]
  • m — mobility and path access
  • p — pace and timing access
  • r — reach and physical interaction
  • s — sensory and perceptual access
  • c — cognitive-load access
  • l — language and communication access
  • e — environmental access
  • g — agency, consent, and control

These values describe access conditions in context. They are not permanent labels attached to individuals.

The current access state can be estimated from declared community needs and environmental conditions:

P(A(t) | D(t), E(t))

D(t) represents deliberately shared community access requirements.

E(t) represents environmental conditions such as slope, curb condition, crossing duration, lighting, weather, seating, route closures, and transit status.

The Community AI objective

x*(t) = arg maxx E[ Participation(x | A(t), E(t)) ]

In plain language:

Choose the action most likely to increase meaningful participation under the current access and environmental conditions.

But participation is not enough by itself. The system must satisfy minimum governance thresholds:

Access(x) ≥ τA
Privacy(x) ≥ τP
Agency(x) ≥ τG

Community AI should not improve one dimension by sacrificing another. Better service cannot justify poor privacy. Efficiency cannot justify reduced agency.

Why the average is not enough

If most people can use a curb and some cannot, the curb does not become accessible because the average person can cross it.

Community AI therefore should not collapse everyone into one typical user.

Kj(t) = Qq{A1j(t), A2j(t), …, Anj(t)}

Using a high quantile instead of only a mean is one way to preserve meaningful access requirements that would otherwise disappear inside an average.

Do not average meaningful access requirements out of existence.

What this means in a neighborhood

A navigation system can distinguish the shortest route from the most usable route.

A crossing can be evaluated against community-established timing requirements.

A neighborhood service can identify repeated reports about the same curb, entrance, crossing, or transit stop and turn those recurring failures into evidence for infrastructure repair.

A local assistant can provide the same information in requested languages, modalities, levels of complexity, and interaction speeds.

The intelligence is not about deciding what kind of person someone is.

It is about determining whether the environment is meeting the access requirements of the people sharing it.

Community AI is governance

The community needs authority over what information is collected, what is retained, what the system is allowed to do, how uncertainty is represented, and how people challenge or override it.

Community AI should favor minimum necessary data, deliberately shared information, local processing where appropriate, transparent rules, accessible explanation, human override, and public accountability.

The community should govern the intelligence.

From the street outward

compute → model → product → user

Community AI reverses the direction:

person → street → community → need → system → infrastructure
Build from the street outward.

At ECHO, the proposition remains:

Access is a condition of correctness.

Community AI adds another:

Participation is the optimization target.

The community becomes an interface.
